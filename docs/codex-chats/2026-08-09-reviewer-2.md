The strongest scientific story may actually be:

> Simple, constrained reaction–diffusion models perform about as well as more complex physics-informed hybrids when assimilating opportunistic spotted-lanternfly reports.

That is interesting because:

- You have 60,142 occurrence records and 20 explicit model ablations.

That last point means you cannot currently claim D05 is “best.” You may be seeing statistical equivalence, threshold-tuning noise, or insufficiently discriminating validation. But a properly tested finding that added complexity provides little benefit would itself be valuable.

What prevents a full research paper today:

- Each model’s threshold is optimized on the same 2021–2025 window used to report performance.
- Monthly backcasts assimilate the reports they display, so visual agreement is not independent validation.
- Unreported cells are pseudo-absences, and urban proximity is a weak reporting-effort proxy.
- There is no spatially blocked, leave-state-out, or genuinely prospective evaluation.
- There are no bootstrap confidence intervals showing whether the 0.1–0.3 score differences mean anything.
- GBIF records represent reporting activity—not unbiased abundance, occupancy, or absence.

The research paper I would build is:

**“Evidence-assimilating reaction–diffusion ensembles for invasion-front mapping from opportunistic occurrence records.”**

The central question would be whether constrained diffusion models produce calibrated, interpretable short-horizon spread estimates under observation bias—and whether neural complexity improves them.

To make that publishable:

1. Lock 2025 or 2026 as a completely untouched test set.
2. Tune thresholds only inside earlier nested validation years.
3. Add spatial block and leave-state-out tests.
4. Compare against simple persistence, distance-buffer, Fisher–KPP, and standard species-distribution baselines.
5. Replace the urban proxy with a real sampling-effort surface where possible.
6. Bootstrap by year, region, and record source to obtain confidence intervals.
7. Report calibration, PR-AUC, recall at fixed survey effort, and spatial error—not only the custom composite score.
8. Keep backcasts, forecasts, interpolation, and observations rigorously separated in figures.

## Major concerns

1. **The central “information available through \(t-1\)” claim is not established.**  
   The data are one GBIF snapshot downloaded in August 2026, while historical states are reconstructed from `eventDate`. A 2021 observation uploaded or corrected in 2024 is therefore treated as known at the 2021 forecast origin. The compact data omit publication/availability timestamps, so this leakage cannot currently be audited.

   Exact fix: reconstruct each origin from archived GBIF snapshots or retain and filter on a defensible record-availability timestamp. Otherwise redefine the task as retrospective event-date reconstruction and remove the operational leakage-resistant claim.

2. **The candidate domain contains guaranteed false negatives.**  
   The occurrence query is restricted to `country=US`, but the rectangular grid extends to 47°N and has no US administrative mask. Canadian land cells are eligible negatives even though Canadian records could never enter the dataset. There are 700 land cells at or above 45°N alone.

   Exact fix: apply a versioned US/state boundary mask or ingest Canadian data consistently. Recompute every fold and figure after masking.

3. **The “spatial-block” analysis is not spatial cross-validation.**  
   Models are trained on earlier observations from the same blocks and neighboring cells. Computing AP within 2° blocks measures conditional within-block discrimination; it does not test geographic transfer. Blocks without positives are discarded using test outcomes, so the analysis also ignores whether a model identifies active regions.

   Exact fix: use leave-state-, leave-county-, or buffered leave-block-out training, with all features and reporting models refit without the held-out region. Describe the present metric only as spatially stratified AP if retained.

4. **Uncertainty is anti-conservative and mislabeled.**  
   The “cluster bootstrap” independently resamples 64 block-years. These are only 20 spatial blocks repeated two to four times, with four shared years and neighboring spatial dependence. A simple year-cluster check widened OG-RDE minus Transport RD from the reported interval to approximately \([-0.037, 0.010]\), reinforcing non-dominance.

   Exact fix: use a two-way block/year bootstrap, spatial moving-block bootstrap, or hierarchical model. Report uncertainty for the primary macro-annual AP and paired annual differences as well.

5. **There is no clean confirmatory test.**  
   The 2022–2025 folds are explicitly development data. The source says the final specification was selected on them. The partial 2026 data are small and non-comparable, and there is no immutable pre-2026 model record proving they were untouched during specification.

   Exact fix: freeze code, parameters, hashes, and predictions before a complete future year, or perform fully nested temporal selection where every modeling choice is made inside each training window.

6. **The “physics” implementation is not yet physically defensible.**  
   `np.roll` imposes toroidal wraparound, allowing diffusion across opposite map edges. Fisher, climate, and transport variants can propagate through water. The grid is evolved isotropically in degrees despite unequal kilometer spacing, and diffusion/growth/jump coefficients lack units, calibration, stability analysis, or sensitivity analysis.

   Exact fix: use a projected metric grid, explicit no-flux/absorbing boundaries, consistent land/water treatment, dimensional parameters tied to published dispersal estimates, and numerical convergence/sensitivity checks.

7. **Presence-only limitations remain central, not peripheral.**  
   First report mixes invasion, observation effort, verification policy, and delayed data publication. The recurrence surface is trained in already-reported cells and cannot identify effort at the frontier. Thus the top-5% result is a retrospective report-prioritization result, not evidence that surveying those cells would detect half of new infestations.

   Exact fix: validate against trap-nights, inspection routes, checklists, or another effort-denominator dataset; otherwise narrow the operational interpretation.

8. **The comparison set cannot support a state-of-the-art claim.**  
   The benchmark compares custom variants sharing one builder, while the project’s own literature review identifies closer published Cox, PoPS, network, and process-model baselines.

   Exact fix: implement at least one published-caliber comparator and add a structured novelty table covering endpoint, data, split, transport representation, and uncertainty. Keep all “SOTA” wording out until demonstrated.
